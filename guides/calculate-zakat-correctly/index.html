How to Calculate Zakat Correctly

Zakat is 2.5% of qualifying wealth that is above the nisab threshold and has been held for a full lunar year. Working it out means listing what you own, checking it against the nisab, and applying the rate. HecTool’s Zakat Calculator does the arithmetic on the values you enter, using the nisab threshold you enter yourself. It is an estimate aid only: nisab values and special cases vary by school of thought, so confirm anything unsure with a trusted scholar. This guide explains the general method; it is not a religious ruling.

Step 1: List your zakatable wealth

List your cash, bank balances, gold, silver and business assets such as trade goods and stock. Use current values for gold and silver, because their prices move. Personal items you use every day, such as your home and car, are generally not counted — but if you are unsure about an asset, ask a scholar rather than guessing.

Step 2: Enter the nisab threshold you follow

Open the Zakat Calculator and enter the nisab threshold you follow. The calculator uses the nisab you enter — it does not set one for you — because thresholds differ by school of thought and by whether they are based on gold or silver. Entering your own figure keeps the estimate aligned with what you follow.

Step 3: Add your values and subtract immediate debts

Enter the value of each type of wealth. Subtract debts that are due now, following the general rule for immediate debts. Debts that are due far in the future are treated differently by different scholars, so if your debts are large or complicated, confirm the treatment before you rely on the result.

Step 4: Read the 2.5% estimate

The calculator applies 2.5% to the total above the nisab and shows the estimated Zakat. If your total is below the nisab, no Zakat is due on it under the general rule. Read the breakdown, not just the final number, so you can see which assets contributed and whether any value looks wrong.

Step 5: Double-check before you pay

Recheck gold and silver values at current prices, confirm the nisab figure you used, and ask a trusted scholar about anything unusual. Then keep a record of what you calculated, what you paid and when, so next year’s calculation starts from a clear history.

Tips for a calculation you can trust

Calculate once a year on the same date, so the lunar-year condition is consistent. Use values from the same day for everything, and do not mix old gold prices with new bank balances. And when in doubt, ask: an estimate tool can do the arithmetic, but only a scholar can settle a question about your specific situation.

Frequently asked questions

Is the rate always 2.5%?

2.5% is the general rate for cash, gold, silver and business assets. Other kinds of wealth have different rules, so confirm anything unusual with a trusted scholar.

What is nisab?

Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth above which Zakat becomes due. Its value depends on the school of thought and on current gold or silver prices, which is why the calculator asks you to enter the threshold you follow.

Does the calculator give a religious ruling?

No. It is an arithmetic aid that applies 2.5% to the values and nisab you enter. Questions about eligibility, debts and special cases belong to a trusted scholar.
