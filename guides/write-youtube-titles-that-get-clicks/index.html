How to Write YouTube Titles That Get Clicks

A viewer decides in about a second, from the title and the thumbnail together, whether to click. The title’s job is to make a clear promise about what the video delivers. A title that overpromises gets the click and loses the viewer, and YouTube notices when people leave early. The honest approach works better: specific, curious and true to the video.

Step 1: Start with the promise, not the cleverness

Write down what the viewer will learn, see or get from the video in one plain sentence. Then put the most important words at the front, because YouTube shortens titles to roughly 60 characters on many screens. A viewer should understand the video from the first five or six words alone.

Step 2: Add honest curiosity

Add one curiosity element: a real question the video answers, a specific number, or an unexpected result. Keep it true. “I tried it for 30 days” works when you did; “You won’t believe what happened” fails when nothing surprising happens. Curiosity that the video pays off builds trust and returning viewers.

Step 3: Get ideas from the title generator

Open the YouTube Title Generator, enter your video topic and tone, and get five clickable title ideas. Treat them as starting points: pick the one closest to your video, then rewrite it in your own words so it sounds like you and matches the video exactly.

Step 4: Score the title with the analyzer

Paste your title into the YouTube Title Analyzer. It scores the title on length, power words, numbers and style, and suggests improvements. The score is a heuristic estimate — a helpful checklist, not a ranking guarantee. A high score never promises views, and a good title can still score modestly.

Step 5: Compare variants and pair with the thumbnail

Write two or three variants, score each one, and choose the clearest rather than the one with the highest number. Then check the title against the thumbnail: together they should tell one story without repeating the same words. If the thumbnail already shows the surprise, the title can carry the context instead.

Tips that keep titles working

Avoid ALL CAPS and strings of exclamation marks — they read as spam and age badly. Use numbers only when they are real. And you can change a title after publishing in YouTube Studio, so a title that underperforms in the first days can be improved without re-uploading the video.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a YouTube title be?

Aim for under 60 characters so it is not cut off on most screens, and put the most important words first so the meaning survives even when it is shortened.

Does a high analyzer score guarantee views?

No. The analyzer score is a heuristic estimate based on length, power words, numbers and style. The video itself, the thumbnail and the audience matter far more than any score.

Can I change a title after publishing?

Yes, in YouTube Studio, without re-uploading. Small improvements in the first days can help, but a title change will not rescue a video viewers do not enjoy.
