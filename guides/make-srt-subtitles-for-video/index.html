How to Make SRT Subtitles for a Video

Captions help viewers who watch with the sound off, viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing, and viewers watching in a second language. They also make a video searchable. HecTool’s Subtitle Caption Maker builds SRT and VTT caption files from lines you type and the start and end times you set. It does not listen to your video or transcribe audio automatically: you write down what is said, and the tool turns it into a correctly formatted captions file.

Step 1: Write down what is said

Play the video and write out the spoken words in short chunks, one sentence or phrase per chunk. Note the rough time each chunk starts. Working from a written list first is much faster than typing and timing at the same time, and it keeps the wording accurate.

Step 2: Open the caption maker and add your cues

Open the Subtitle Caption Maker and add one cue per chunk. Each cue has a start time and an end time in minutes and seconds, plus the text itself. Add cues in the order they are spoken, and keep the list in the same order as the video.

Step 3: Time each cue to the speech

Set the start time to the moment the speech begins and the end time to just after it finishes. Keep each cue on screen for one to six seconds, with no more than two lines and roughly 42 characters per line, so viewers can read it comfortably. Leave a small gap between cues instead of letting them touch.

Step 4: Fix overlaps and read the whole file through

If a cue starts before the previous one ends, the tool warns you. Fix these overlaps by shortening the earlier cue’s end time. Then read every line once more for spelling, especially names, and play the video with the captions on to check that the words match the speech.

Step 5: Download and test the captions file

Download the SRT file for most players and for YouTube, or the VTT file for web players. Test it before you publish: open the video and the captions file together in a player such as VLC, or upload both to YouTube Studio and preview. Naming the captions file the same as the video file helps many players find it automatically.

Tips for captions people actually read

Write what is said, not a summary, and keep filler words only when they carry meaning. Mark important non-speech sounds in square brackets, like [music] or [laughter], so viewers who cannot hear them still follow the scene. And if the timing drifts halfway through, check one cue in the middle of the video: a single wrong time early on is the usual cause.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between SRT and VTT?

Both hold the same cues. SRT works in most video players and on YouTube, while VTT is the standard for web players. The maker can download either format from the same cues.

Does the tool transcribe my audio automatically?

No. You type the spoken lines and set the times yourself. That keeps the wording accurate, including names and technical terms that automatic tools often get wrong.

What if the captions drift out of sync?

Check a cue in the middle of the video and compare its time with the speech. One wrong time early in the list shifts everything after it, so fix the first wrong cue and re-check.
