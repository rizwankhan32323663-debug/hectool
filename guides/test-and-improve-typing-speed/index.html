How to Test and Improve Your Typing Speed

Typing speed is measured in WPM — words per minute — and accuracy matters just as much as speed. Fast typing full of errors is slower in real work than steady typing that needs no corrections. HecTool’s Typing Speed Test is a 60-second test with WPM and accuracy results, using English or Urdu passages, and it runs entirely in your browser.

Step 1: Open the test and choose a passage

Open the Typing Speed Test and choose an English or Urdu passage. Use the same keyboard and sitting position you normally use, and pick a quiet moment: the first result should reflect your everyday typing, not a perfect day.

Step 2: Type the passage for 60 seconds

Type the passage as it appears, at your natural pace, for the full 60 seconds. Do not watch the clock, and do not stop to fix every small mistake — the test scores what you actually produce, including errors, so typing naturally gives the most useful baseline.

Step 3: Read both numbers, not just WPM

When the minute ends, read your WPM and your accuracy together. High speed with low accuracy means you are outrunning your fingers, while high accuracy with low speed means you have room to push the pace. Write both numbers down with the date: they are your starting point.

Step 4: Practise in short daily sessions

Practise for 10 to 15 minutes a day rather than one long session a week. Keep your fingers on the home row, look at the screen instead of the keys, and slow down until accuracy improves — speed follows accuracy, not the other way round. For Urdu passages, practise with the keyboard layout you will actually use, so the finger memory transfers.

Step 5: Retest weekly under the same conditions

Retest once a week with the same keyboard, posture and time of day, and compare with your written log. A trend over four to six weeks is meaningful; a single day’s score is not, because tiredness, a new keyboard or a hard passage can all move it. If the trend is flat, spend a week on accuracy alone before pushing speed again.

Tips for steady improvement

Rest your hands between sessions and stop if your wrists ache — pain is a signal, not a target. Learn the common words in your language so your fingers anticipate them, and practise real sentences rather than random letters. Small daily practice beats occasional long sessions, every time.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good typing speed?

As a rough general guide, around 40 WPM is average, 60 WPM is good, and 80 WPM and above is fast. These are rough guides only — what matters is whether your speed is enough for the work you do.

Does accuracy matter more than speed?

Both matter, but accuracy comes first. Errors cost the time it takes to find and fix them, so a fast test with many mistakes is slower in real work than steady, accurate typing.

How long does improvement take?

With short daily practice, most people see a real change over several weeks. There is no fixed timetable — regular practice matters far more than any single session.
