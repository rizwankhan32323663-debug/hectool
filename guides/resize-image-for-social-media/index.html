How to Resize an Image for Social Media

Every platform crops photos differently. A square that looks perfect on Instagram gets cut off as a channel banner, and a wide banner shrinks into a tiny strip in a feed. The fix is simple: make one correctly sized file for each place you post. HecTool’s Social Media Image Resizer runs in your browser, so your photo never leaves your device while you resize it.

Step 1: Open the resizer and add your photo

Open the Social Media Image Resizer and add your photo. Start from the largest original you have, not from a copy that has already been resized or compressed. Shrinking a large photo keeps it sharp; enlarging a small one makes it soft.

Step 2: Pick the preset for the platform

Choose the preset for where the photo will be posted. The common sizes are: Instagram post 1080 by 1080, Instagram story 1080 by 1920, YouTube thumbnail 1280 by 720, YouTube channel banner 2560 by 1440, X header 1500 by 500, and Facebook cover 820 by 312. If the size you need is not listed, use the custom size option and type the width and height yourself.

Step 3: Choose fit or fill, and check the preview

Fit keeps the whole photo visible and adds empty space around it, while fill crops the photo until it covers the whole frame. Fill usually looks better in feeds, but check the live preview first: faces, logos and text near the edges can be cut off. If something important is cropped, switch to fit, or pick a different preset and compare the two results.

Step 4: Pick a format and download

For photographs, JPG gives the smallest files. For graphics, screenshots or images with text, PNG stays sharper. WebP is usually the smallest of all when the platform accepts it. Download the file, then repeat steps 2 to 4 for every other platform, so each post gets its own correctly sized file.

Step 5: Check the result before you post

Open each downloaded file and look at it at full size. Check that faces are not cut, text is readable, and the image looks sharp. Also check the file size against the platform’s upload limit — if a file is too large, run it through the Image Compressor or switch the format with the Image Format Converter.

Tips that save a second attempt

Keep the original photo untouched and always resize a copy. Make the banner and cover versions from the same original, because profile pictures and buttons overlap the edges of banners on phones. And re-check sizes once in a while: platforms change their recommended dimensions, and an old preset can quietly go out of date.

Frequently asked questions

Will resizing blur my photo?

Shrinking a photo keeps it sharp. Enlarging a photo beyond its original size is what makes it soft, so always start from the largest original you have.

Do I need a different file for each platform?

Yes. Each platform displays a different shape and size, so one file cannot fit them all. Make one download per platform from the same original photo.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The resizer runs in your browser, and your photo never leaves your device.
