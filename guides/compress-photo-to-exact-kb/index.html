How to Compress a Photo to an Exact KB Size

Many forms, job portals and messaging apps reject photos over a set file size — often 100 KB, 200 KB or 1 MB. HecTool's image tools run in your browser, so your photo never leaves your device while you work on it.

Step 1: Open the Image Compressor and add your photo

Open the Image Compressor and choose your photo. It accepts JPG, PNG and WebP. Start from your original photo, not from a copy that has already been compressed, so you keep as much quality as possible.

Step 2: Pick the right format

If the place you are sending the photo to accepts WebP, choose WebP — it usually gives the smallest file at the same visible quality. If it needs JPG (for older forms and portals), choose JPG. PNG suits screenshots, but is usually the largest format for photos. You can switch formats any time with the Image Format Converter.

Step 3: Lower the quality in small steps

Set the quality to around 80 and download the result. Check the downloaded file's size in your files app. If it is still over your target, compress again at 70, then 60 — small steps protect quality.

Step 4: If it is still too big, resize the photo

Dimensions matter more than quality. A 4000-pixel-wide photo is far larger than the same photo at 1600 pixels wide, and most upload forms display photos much smaller than that. Use the Image Resizer to reduce the width — try 1600 pixels for a general upload, or 1200 for a small profile photo — keeping the aspect ratio locked, then compress the resized copy again.

Step 5: Check the result before you send it

Open the final file and look at it at full size. Faces, text and fine detail should still be clear. Aim to land just under the size limit rather than far below it, so you keep the best quality the limit allows.

Tips that save a second attempt

Compress the copy, never the only original. Work down in steps. If the photo looks blocky, go back one step. And check the required format before you start: a perfect WebP file will not help if the form only accepts JPG.

Frequently asked questions

Will compression blur my photo?

A little quality loss is normal, but at quality 70–85 and sensible dimensions most photos look the same on screen. If detail looks soft, raise the quality one step.

What if the limit is very small, like 20 KB?

Use WebP, reduce the width to 800–1000 pixels, and lower quality in steps. Very small limits always cost some visible quality — prioritise the subject staying recognisable.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The image tools run in your browser, and your photo never leaves your device.
