How to Make a Free Freelance Invoice

A clear invoice gets paid faster than a vague one. The client should see at a glance who the invoice is from, what the work was, how much is owed and by when. HecTool’s Invoice Generator builds a clean invoice with line items, tax and totals in your browser — preview it, print it, or download it as text, with no sign-up.

Step 1: Add your details and the client’s details

Open the Invoice Generator and enter your name or business name and contact details, plus the client’s name and contact details. Use the same business name and details on every invoice: consistency makes your records — and the client’s — easy to search later.

Step 2: Number and date the invoice

Give the invoice a unique number and an issue date, and set the due date or payment terms, for example 7 or 14 days. Number invoices in sequence and never reuse a number: if you ever need to correct one, issue a new number rather than editing the old one silently.

Step 3: Itemise the work, line by line

Add one line per piece of work, with a specific description, the quantity or hours, the rate and the amount. “Three blog posts, 1,200 words each” is clearer than “writing work”, and specific lines are rarely questioned. Group related work under clear headings if the invoice is long.

Step 4: Check totals and tax

Check the subtotal, add tax only at the rate that actually applies to you, and confirm the final total. If you are not sure whether tax applies to your work, check your local rules before sending the invoice — this tool is a template, not tax advice. Write the currency in a note on the invoice if it could be unclear to the client.

Step 5: Preview, print or download, then send

Preview the finished invoice and read it once for typos in names, numbers and dates. Then print it, or download it as text and keep a copy for your records. To send a PDF, use your browser’s print dialog and choose Save as PDF. Send it to the person who approves payment, not just to a general inbox, and note the date you sent it.

Tips for getting paid on time

State how you accept payment, and put any late-payment terms on the invoice only if you actually apply them. Send the invoice the day the work is accepted, while it is fresh in the client’s mind. And keep a simple log of invoice numbers, clients, amounts and payment dates — chasing a late payment is far easier with records.

Frequently asked questions

Is this invoice a legal or tax document?

It is a template for a professional-looking invoice, not legal or tax advice. Check your local tax and business rules for what your invoices must include.

Can I edit the invoice after downloading it?

Regenerate it in the tool with the same invoice number and keep one version as the record. Never quietly change an invoice a client has already received — issue a corrected copy instead.

What currency does the invoice use?

The amounts are the numbers you type, so they are in whatever currency you agreed with the client. Write the currency in a note on the invoice if there is any chance of confusion.
